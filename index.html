Why a flatbed makes the difference

Carried on the bed or dragged on two wheels: here is how the two methods compare.

How we do it

Flatbed recovery

Your vehicle is driven or winched onto the bed and strapped down.

  • All wheels are off the road for the whole journey
  • Suitable for damaged or non-rolling vehicles
  • Kinder to gearboxes, drivelines and tyres on long trips
  • The same truck carries tractors, implements and site equipment

Hook-and-chain towing

The vehicle is lifted at one end and dragged on the other wheels.

  • Wheels turn on the road, adding wear to tyres and drivetrain
  • Poor choice for automatics and 4x4s
  • Not suitable for crash-damaged or low-clearance vehicles
  • Cannot move tractors or heavy machinery

Broken down? Do this while you wait

Six simple steps that keep you and your passengers safe until the truck arrives.

Switch on your hazards

Make your vehicle as visible as possible, especially at night or in poor light.

Get off the road

Move as far onto the shoulder as you safely can, away from passing traffic.

Passengers out safely

If it is safe, let everyone wait well clear of the road, behind a barrier or off the verge.

Set up a warning triangle

If you carry one, place it well behind your vehicle so drivers see you in time.

Never stand in traffic

Do not work between your vehicle and moving cars. Wait where you are protected.

Send us your location

Share a WhatsApp location pin and a photo of the vehicle so we can find you faster.

If anyone is injured or in immediate danger, contact the emergency services first, then call us.
